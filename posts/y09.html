The Carrington Event

1859: the Sun hit Earth so hard, telegraph wires caught fire.

Watch it on @agenda2027.daily

September 1st, 1859. An astronomer sketching sunspots sees two bright flashes on the Sun.

His name was Richard Carrington. It was the first solar flare ever recorded.

Within a day, the strongest geomagnetic storm on record hit the Earth.

Telegraph wires sparked. Operators got shocks. Some kept sending messages with their batteries disconnected.

Auroras lit up the sky close to the tropics.

In 2013, Lloyd's estimated a storm like that today could cost the U.S. up to two point six trillion dollars.

It has happened before. Our power grid has never faced one.
